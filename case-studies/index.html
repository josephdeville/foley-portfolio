01 · Cortex · First in-house GTM engineer

Deep Research + Hermes

Account executives were booking meetings and walking in cold with generic notes, no buyer read, and no close plan. Marketing and outbound copy was either safe-and-bland or slow to produce. I built two connected agents to run as a single loop.

Deep Research · Pre-call brief

Triggered on any external calendar booking. Pulls company signals (funding, hiring, tech stack, news), attendee history, and every prior CRM touch. Infers behavioral buying style (decisive vs. consensus, numbers vs. narrative) and posts a one-page close plan to Slack 30 minutes before the call.

Hermes · Learning outbound

Turns the research packet into persona-tuned cold emails, follow-ups, and LinkedIn notes cloned to the sender's voice. Generates parallel variants and re-weights prompt framing as reply, meeting, and stage-progression events flow back into LangFuse.

Why it learns instead of running fixed rules: Scoring, routing, and enrichment are schema tasks. Deciding which angle lands with a skeptical VP of Engineering versus a risk-first legal ops buyer requires continuous feedback from replies, booked meetings, and pipeline progression.

Impact: Part of the Cortex GTM systems credited with $1.57M in pipeline influence. Saved AEs 30 to 45 minutes of manual prep per call, with variant performance tracked per persona and a hard kill rule at 500 sends.

Stack: TypeScript, Python, CrewAI, LangChain, LangFuse, Trigger.dev, Clay, Salesforce, Slack.

02 · Cortex · Pipeline systems

Signal-driven BDR lead feed

BDRs were working static Salesforce account lists built a quarter earlier. By the time a rep reached an account, the hiring, funding, or stack change that made it timely was cold. Static lists also cannot be A/B tested like signal hypotheses.

  • Multi-source Clay waterfalls: Pulled daily from LinkedIn Sales Navigator, ZoomInfo, Clearbit, and custom scrapers tracking job reqs, stack changes, and funding events.
  • Dynamic ICP scoring: Scored accounts against what was converting that month rather than a frozen spreadsheet rubric.
  • Hard CRM suppression: Matched against Salesforce and internal records to suppress active sequences, existing customers, recent closed-lost deals, and rep-owned accounts before anything queued.
  • Workflow delivery: Routed top accounts into Salesforce queues and morning Slack cards with the exact trigger attached so reps knew why the account surfaced today.

Scale & impact: 10,000+ leads scored across SMB and Mid-Market, feeding 3 BDRs daily, saving ~3 hours per rep per week of manual list hygiene, and contributing to $1.57M in influenced pipeline across 17 Cortex builds. Signal categories were re-ranked monthly by booked-meeting conversion.

Stack: Clay, Python, Trigger.dev, Salesforce (Certified Platform Administrator), HubSpot, Marketo, Slack.

03 · Rainforest QA · Sending infrastructure

Email deliverability · 40% bounce drop

Hard bounces were climbing across multiple marketing and outbound platforms, putting domain reputation on a short path to blocklists. Unverified list imports, 12-month-stale re-engagement sends, and siloed bounce logs meant a hard bounce in one tool kept getting mailed from another.

  • Unified suppression source of truth: Webhook handlers and scheduled Python/SQL sync jobs ensured a bounce or opt-out on any platform suppressed across every sending tool immediately.
  • Mailbox and receiver throttling: Enforced per-mailbox daily caps, ramp curves on new or reactivated inboxes, and receiver-specific pacing across Gmail, Microsoft 365, and Yahoo.
  • Authentication & health checks: Locked in SPF, DKIM, DMARC alignment and isolated tracking domains before scaling send volume.

Impact: Hard bounces dropped 40%, sender reputation recovered inside the quarter, and centralized suppression became the shared guardrail underneath every sequence and nurture program afterward.

Stack: Python, SQL, webhook workers, SPF/DKIM/DMARC monitoring, multi-platform marketing and outbound senders.

04 · Post-Cortex consulting

Outbound infrastructure · Anchor Browser & Crux

Both consulting engagements started from my own outbound motion, then expanded into building the production GTM infrastructure behind their pipeline.

Anchor Browser · Browser automation SaaS

Stood up a 22-mailbox sending pool across 5 domains (Google + Azure), fit-gated enrichment across 55+ microsegments, security-scanner click filtering (~89% of raw clicks were non-human bots), and ~1.7–1.9% positive reply benchmarks.

Crux · Payments & finance

Built ICP-specific enrichment waterfalls and HubSpot attribution for high-consideration finance and payments buyers so reply quality and meeting conversion were measurable by microsegment.

Experiment discipline: Every campaign shipped with a pre-agreed kill rule. At 500 sends per variant, anything below 0.5% positive reply was killed and rotated; winners scaled.

Stack: TypeScript, Python, Clay, Smartlead, Instantly, HubSpot, Trigger.dev, custom sending and tracking domains.

05 · Operating model

How I work · GTM engineering principles

One foot in pipeline, one in process. Every week balances builds that create pipeline this quarter (signal feeds, scoring models, outbound experiments) with builds that give reps and marketers hours back (auto-enriched briefs, silent-deal nudges, automated suppression hygiene).

Measure in forecast language, not activity language. "Agent shipped" is not a metric. Every system is instrumented against the pipeline event it is meant to move, with RevOps attribution agreed before code is written.

Prioritize by reversibility and blast radius. High-reversibility, low-blast-radius experiments (new signal trigger, single-rep workflow) ship in days with a 30-day keep-or-kill gate. High-blast-radius or compliance-sensitive changes (CRM routing, domain-wide sending rules, customer data) pilot on a slice with explicit guardrails and evals first.

Use learning loops only where judgment varies. Routers, scorers, and enrichers stay deterministic against strict schemas. Copy framing, buyer persona reads, and signal weighting earn feedback loops tied to replies and stage conversion.

Write it down early. Every build starts with a one-paragraph problem statement, target pipeline metric, and kill rule, and closes with a short post-ship readout of what moved and what got cut.
